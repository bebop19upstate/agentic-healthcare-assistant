Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a long-term condition in which the kidneys gradually lose their ability to filter waste and excess fluid from the blood. CKD is classified into five stages based on estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), with stage 1 being mild kidney damage with normal function and stage 5 (end-stage renal disease) requiring dialysis or transplant. Common causes include diabetes, high blood pressure, and glomerulonephritis. Early stages are often asymptomatic, which is why routine blood and urine testing is important for at-risk patients, particularly those over 60 or with a family history of kidney disease.
