Treatment for chronic kidney disease focuses on slowing progression and managing complications, since kidney damage is generally irreversible. Standard approaches include blood pressure control using ACE inhibitors or ARBs, dietary sodium and protein restriction, and careful management of blood sugar in diabetic patients. SGLT2 inhibitors, originally developed for diabetes, have shown significant benefit in slowing CKD progression even in non-diabetic patients by reducing pressure within the kidney's filtering units. Patients in later stages may require erythropoietin-stimulating agents to manage anemia, and phosphate binders to control mineral imbalances. Regular monitoring of eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) helps track disease progression and guide treatment adjustments.
